New to the numbers? A quick guide

Revenue is what the business sells. Operating margin shows the profit left from operations per dollar of sales. Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations after capital spending. FCF yield compares that cash to the price of the business’s equity. TTM means the latest four reported quarters. High growth can be valuable, but look at the cash and capital needed to achieve it.

How to read this comparison

Lime cells mark the leader for that metric within the selected group, including ties. There is no overall buy/sell score. Growth, margins, ROIC proxy, and FCF yield favor higher values; EV/EBITDA and net debt/EBITDA favor lower values. Negative net debt means net cash. Nonpositive earnings or EBITDA produce unavailable multiples. Different business mixes, fiscal periods, accounting policies, and leverage can limit comparability.

ROIC proxy = TTM operating income × (1 − assumed 21% tax) / average invested capital at the year-ago and latest available quarter. Invested capital = book equity + total debt − cash & short-term investments. Cash-rich and financial businesses can make this measure unhelpful. Banks, insurers, and REITs need sector-specific methods.

What the numbers cannot tell you

Read customer and supplier concentration, revenue recognition, related-party transactions, auditor opinions, internal controls, legal proceedings, debt covenants, guarantees, and purchase commitments. Compare risk-factor changes across annual and quarterly filings. Screen ratios do not establish fraud or capture every off-balance-sheet obligation.

SEC Investor.gov: how to read a 10-K ↗ explains where to find risk factors, management analysis, and audited statements. For foreign issuers, also review 20-F and 6-K filings. A filing’s presence does not mean it contains a negative finding.

What this model assumes

Unlevered free cash flow = EBIT − cash taxes on positive EBIT + depreciation & amortization − capital expenditures − change in working capital. Growth, EBIT margin, and CapEx/revenue transition linearly across five years. Terminal cash flow grows from year 5 at the terminal growth rate and is discounted at WACC. Equity value adds cash and subtracts debt; a negative modeled equity value is floored at zero per share.

No future dilution, acquisitions, asset sales, tax-loss carryforwards, lease adjustments, or other claims are modeled. Terminal reinvestment remains implicit in year-5 CapEx, D&A, and working capital; review those assumptions for consistency. Defaults are a starting point, not a forecast. The method is generally unsuitable for financial institutions and REITs.
